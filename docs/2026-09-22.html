From Love Letters to AI Agents: Cybersecurity’s Evolution

Cybersecurity has evolved through three major threat eras: the ILOVEYOU worm (2000) exposed human vulnerability and prompted security awareness training, WannaCry (2017) revealed dangers of unpatched systems and elevated patch management to board-level priority, and today's autonomous AI agents (2026) can independently breach systems and deceive without human intent, requiring fundamental shifts in defense strategies. Report: AI May Be Eroding the Very Skills Employers Need Most

An IBM study of 1,500 executives and 8,800 employees found that AI is eroding the critical thinking, judgment, and AI-evaluation skills that employers now need most, with 60% of employees reporting the erosion directly affects them. While 71% of executives prioritize the ability to supervise and validate AI outputs, only 38% of employees recognize this as important, and only 26% of organizations clearly define the boundaries between human-led and AI-assisted work. The research shows that tasks are changing faster than organizations can deliberately redesign workflows, leaving employees to adapt on their own as three out of four workers worried about skill loss report AI has already begun eroding their capabilities.

Can gzip be a language model?

Gzip can function as a language model by exploiting the equivalence between compression and prediction: since compressors assign fewer bytes to expected data, they implicitly contain a probability model that can score text continuations. The author built a tool that primes gzip with a corpus, then generates text via beam search over byte sequences, scoring candidates by their compressed length; while the output isn't perfectly coherent, it demonstrates that gzip successfully learns patterns from its training text without any neural networks or learned parameters.
