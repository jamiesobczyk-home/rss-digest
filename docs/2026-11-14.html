Building strong safety evaluations

Anthropic outlines recommendations for building reliable AI safety evaluations, which measure a model's propensity to cause harm rather than its capabilities. Drawing from psychology's replication crisis and reforms like preregistration, Anthropic emphasizes that strong safety evals require multidisciplinary teams combining domain and methods expertise, and must be conducted in monitored sandboxes to prevent unintended harm during testing.

BYOH: Bring Your Own Harness with Cisco IQ, for partners

Cisco's Carlos Pereira argues that AI harnesses—software agents that automate complex work tasks—are following the trajectory of "Bring Your Own Device" (BYOD), with employees increasingly bringing personal AI tools they trust into their workplace. Drawing parallels to how smartphones forced enterprises to evolve their security and identity management, he predicts "Bring Your Own Harness" (BYOH) will require companies to rethink architecture, policy, and identity management to safely accommodate trusted harnesses like Claude Code and Cursor. Beyond security, the core challenge for enterprises will be managing trusted context rather than just device control.

Elephants found to be increasingly isolated leading to fractured social networks

UC San Diego researchers tracking over 400 Asian elephants in Sri Lanka for 12 years found that population decline has fractured their social networks, with the animals shifting from large, densely connected communities to smaller, isolated groups. This social "tipping point" raises concerns because elephants depend on their networks to forage, share information, and rear young. The researchers warn that accelerating losses from deaths and emigration, combined with few new individuals joining populations, threaten the animals' survival and social cohesion.
