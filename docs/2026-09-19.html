AWS Architecture Blog · 4:33 PM

ReadyOn’s Four Walls of tenant isolation on Amazon EKS

ReadyOn implements a four-layered isolation model on Amazon EKS to secure multi-tenant workloads: namespace isolation with RBAC, dedicated compute node pools via Karpenter, VPC-level security groups, and per-tenant Aurora databases with short-lived credentials. This defense-in-depth approach ensures that crossing a tenant boundary requires simultaneously defeating isolation mechanisms at the API, scheduler, network, and data layers rather than relying on any single boundary. The architecture has been validated through adversarial testing with no successful cross-tenant paths identified.

AWS Architecture Blog · 9:35 AM

How CSIRO built scalable, cost-optimized genomic variant querying on AWS

CSIRO developed Serverless Beacon (sBeacon), a scalable serverless implementation of the GA4GH Beacon protocol for querying genomic variant data using AWS services (Lambda, S3, DynamoDB, Athena), enabling queries across hundreds of millions of individuals at approximately $0.40/month operational cost with response times around 5 seconds. The system eliminates the need for heavy data ingestion by directly consuming standard VCF files and keeping genomic data at its source while indexing only metadata, with a case study showing ingestion of chromosome 1 data from 2,504 samples in 18 seconds for under 1 cent. sBeacon implements zero-trust security, role-based access control, and federated decentralization, making it accessible for smaller institutions and enabling cross-institutional genomic data discovery.

GPT-6 Astra Solves a WWI German Radio Cipher

GPT-6 Astra decrypted a previously unsolved ADFGVX-encoded German radio message from November 27, 1918, revealing communications about an English cruiser's arrival in Sevastopol and an allied squadron's follow-up movement. The model solved it by identifying that "TRUPPENVERSCHIEBUNG" was the encryption key used for this message, despite being documented as adopted only on December 9, 1918. The decoded message's historical details were verified through HMS Canterbury's original logs, which confirm the cruiser arrived in Sevastopol on November 24 and an allied squadron followed on November 26.

Hacker News · 1:28 AM

If math is more than proof, we need to better celebrate the rest of it

Mathematician Grant Sanderson argues that as AI becomes capable of generating proofs, academia should elevate "motivated explanations"—work focused on clarifying how mathematicians think about problems—to the same status as proof generation, since the true goal of mathematics is advancing human understanding. He proposes formally defining motivated explanations as narratives that explain "how would you think of that?" through discovery-based storytelling, contrasting them with proofs that begin with definitions and require each step to follow logically. Recognizing such work would better reflect what mathematicians actually contribute and prevent misconceptions that AI has rendered the field obsolete.

Human brain is two separate organs, Stanford Medicine-led research finds

Stanford Medicine researchers discovered that the human brain is two distinct organs that evolved independently, rather than a single unified organ as previously believed. The forebrain handles higher-level thinking, while the hindbrain controls essential automatic functions like breathing and heart rate. This finding explains why scientists have struggled to grow hindbrain neurons in laboratories and enables new research into brain stem diseases like spinal muscular atrophy and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.
