Doomscrolling at work wastes time, but the real cost is what happens after

Research from Texas A&M University found that employees who doomscroll at work—obsessively consuming negative news—ruminate on the distressing content they encounter, which reduces their work engagement even after they stop scrolling. The effect is particularly pronounced in people with higher levels of neuroticism. While doomscrolling appears to be merely a productivity loss, the hidden cost is sustained mental preoccupation with the negative information, driven by attempts to manage anxiety during uncertain situations.

Wi-Fi 8 is the first wireless upgrade in years that isn't chasing speed

Wi-Fi 8 marks a departure from previous upgrades by prioritizing reliability over raw speed, maintaining the same maximum data rate as Wi-Fi 7 while targeting improvements in real-world performance. The IEEE-dubbed "Ultra High Reliability" standard aims to reduce interference and handle multiple connected devices more effectively, with goals to increase throughput by 25%, reduce latency by 25%, and decrease packet loss by 25%. The End of an Athlon

A large chunk of silicon broke off an Athlon XP processor when its heatsink was removed, revealing a micro-crack that fractured under pressure. The incident exemplifies why Intel and AMD abandoned flip-chip PGA processors with exposed silicon around 2000—they were extremely fragile and susceptible to damage from uneven heatsink installation forces. Both companies switched to lidded CPU packaging for superior durability and mechanical resilience.
