Microsoft's Jacob Andreou on AI's product gap

Microsoft's Jacob Andreou argues that AI's core problem isn't model capability but the lack of useful products built around them—a lesson Microsoft learned by removing Copilot from low-value Windows features when it found usage matters more than traffic. The new Copilot addresses this gap by integrating chat, code-building, and AI agents into one workplace "OS" that routes tasks to the best-suited model rather than betting on a single provider. Andreou emphasizes that true AI-native work requires rethinking workflows rather than just speeding up tasks, while agents need human approval only for irreversible actions.

"Robot" Is a Social Construct: Why Your Dishwasher Is Not a Robot

The term "robot" is a social construct, not a technical definition—we apply it to automated machines that feel new or unsettling, not based on their actual capabilities. Coined by Karel Čapek in his 1920 play, "robot" became sci-fi shorthand for technologies that trigger human anxieties about jobs, consciousness, and what it means to be human. A dishwasher, despite being automated and increasingly smart, isn't called a robot because it doesn't provoke these deeper existential concerns.

IRCv3

IRCv3 is an open, extensible chat protocol featuring backwards-compatible extensions including standardized SASL authentication, message metadata, away notifications, and timestamping for improved history playback. The IRCv3 Working Group continues developing standards for account registration, chat history access, automatic secure connections, and Unicode nickname support. The specification is maintained collaboratively by organizations including InspIRCd, Irssi, WeeChat, HexChat, and ZNC.
