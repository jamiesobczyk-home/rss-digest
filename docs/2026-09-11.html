Two zones or three? A design framework for zone-resilient Azure workloads

Zone resiliency decisions should be made component-by-component rather than applying a uniform approach across entire workloads; two zones typically meet single-zone failure objectives for stateless and easily-replicated systems, while three zones are essential only for quorum-based designs, highest-durability data stores, or components that cannot sustain required capacity with two zones. Neijuan

Neijuan (内卷), a Chinese economic term meaning "to curl inwards," describes a system where increased effort and competition fail to produce proportional gains in productivity or output, resulting in diminishing returns. Originally developed as "involution" by American anthropologist Alexander Goldenweiser in 1937 and later studied by Clifford Geertz in agricultural contexts, the concept has been applied to explain overproduction, price wars, and uneven resource distribution in modern markets. At the personal level, neijuan reflects a lifestyle of intense competition that leaves people overworked and stressed despite little meaningful progress.
