Working at the frontier shapes expectations for the future

Anthropic reports that its employees, who work at the frontier of AI adoption, have dramatically different expectations about AI's future impact on work compared to the general public, despite sharing similar economic models about labor markets. Within Anthropic, AI agents now work six times more hours than employees, driving increased productivity, but this direct experience leads 91% of staff to expect AI to make Nobel-level discoveries by 2030 (versus 39% of US adults) and 50%+ to expect AI could do 80% of their job by 2030 (versus only 5% of Americans). The gap between public and employee expectations stems not from different theories about AI's effects, but from fundamentally different experiences with what current AI systems can actually do.

Anthropic Research · 7:00 PM

Claude reveals ECDSA curve origins

Anthropic's Claude AI resolved a decades-long cryptography mystery by determining that NIST's ECDSA curve seeds were generated using a simple C library random-number generator initialized with Unix timestamps, not the suspected hash algorithm. The discovery indicates the curves were likely generated without backdoors or intentional weaknesses, addressing long-standing concerns about whether the NSA-provided parameters contained deliberate flaws.

Designing more efficient quantum circuits with Claude

Claude optimized eight quantum circuit designs from chemistry, optimization, and physics simulation, finding solutions that improve on the best published circuits by up to an order of magnitude or more. These improvements substantially reduce the quantum resources required for useful applications, suggesting practical quantum computers could arrive sooner than previously anticipated. The work demonstrates Claude can effectively optimize quantum algorithms given only plain-language descriptions of what each circuit must compute.

Anthropic Research · 7:00 PM

The AI expectations gap

A survey comparing US adults to Anthropic staff reveals sharp disagreements about AI's future: while both groups agree on current AI capabilities, Anthropic staff expect much faster progress and far greater workplace automation, with 91% expecting Nobel-level discoveries by 2030 versus 39% of adults. Staff members anticipate AI will perform 75% of their current work by 2030 compared to 28% for general US workers, and among both groups, those expecting more AI automation of their own jobs forecast higher unemployment rates.

Is MCP becoming the common foundation of smart, decentralised search?

MCP, an open protocol now overseen by the Linux Foundation, enables AI assistants to fetch information directly from distributed servers rather than relying on frozen training data, fundamentally shifting search from centralized indexing to decentralized retrieval. Each publisher keeps its data at home and serves it on demand, providing up-to-date answers with verifiable sources, licenses, and proper attribution. While adoption by major assistants and developer tools is rapid, full implementation still requires standards for citation, trust verification, and privacy-respecting measurement.

Astronomers Discover the First Green Star

Astronomers have identified WD J1330+6435, a white dwarf located 326 light-years from Earth, as possibly the first genuinely green star. The star acquired its green hue after accreting rocky material from an orbiting planet, enriching its atmosphere with calcium, lithium, and sodium—the sodium traces absorb the orange part of the spectrum, leaving a ghostly green glow. The discovery has been published in the journal RAS Techniques and Instruments.
