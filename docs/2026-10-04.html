Why don't more developers “use the platform”?

Despite advocates championing web platform features, developers often reach for JavaScript libraries and frameworks instead due to historical browser limitations, familiarity with npm ecosystems, superior third-party documentation, and the simple fact that building custom solutions is often more enjoyable than learning platform APIs. The divide grew partly because React and similar tools abstracted away platform APIs in favor of more ergonomic frameworks, while libraries like jQuery once filled gaps that browsers were slow to address. Rather than pure laziness or performance concerns, developers frequently prefer the fun and control of building their own solutions, even when the platform could provide them more efficiently.

This Virus Stole a Human Gene and Won't Let Go of It

Scientists discovered that molluscum contagiosum virus carries the human gene BC200, which it stole from our ancestors roughly 100,000 years ago and may now use to infect us. BC200 is important for brain function and can jump around and insert itself into genomes, and it is abnormally expressed in some tumors and overexpressed in Alzheimer's patients' brains. Researchers plan to investigate whether the virus exploits this stolen gene to manipulate human cells and what role BC200 plays in disease development, including cancer and neurological conditions.

Physicists Identify 'Octupolar' Magnetism, With Implications for Quantum Technologies

University of Toronto physicists have developed a new method for observing "octupolar" magnetic states—patterns where particles behave as if they have eight magnetic poles rather than the conventional two—using light to probe atomic vibrations. By directing rotating light at magnetic materials, the team detected previously hidden magnetic order, providing a critical tool for uncovering and manipulating these quantum states. The discovery opens pathways for applying multipolar magnetism to next-generation data storage and computing technologies.
