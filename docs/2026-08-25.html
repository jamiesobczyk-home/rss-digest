How a global payment processor preserved AWS RAM shares and Lake Formation permissions during an AWS Organizations migration

A global payment processor migrated 382 AWS accounts between organizations using a "bridge-share pattern" to preserve AWS RAM resource shares and Lake Formation permissions that would normally break during the move. The solution creates temporary external resource shares before migration, accepts them from consumer accounts, then restores the original organization-bound shares afterward to maintain a single authoritative permission source. The company successfully migrated 378 accounts with no customer disruption and AWS published the validated pattern and automation for other organizations to use.

Windows Backdoor 'Sleepwalker' Hides in Memory Until Activated by a 'Magic Packet'

A newly discovered Windows backdoor called Sleepwalker remains dormant in memory until activated by a specially crafted "magic packet," then executes commands using its own custom 23-instruction language. The malware masquerades as Microsoft's dpapi.dll and is injected through ESET Management Agent, avoiding detection by sending no outbound traffic and only listening for specific incoming packets. This approach is attributed to a well-resourced, targeted operation rather than opportunistic malware.

Screen viewing time from age 1 to 8 years and subsequent academic performance

A longitudinal study of 502 children found that higher screen viewing time from ages 1–8 years was associated with poorer academic performance at age 9 and lower working memory at 10.5 years, with the strongest effects at ages 1, 1.5, and 6 years. Cumulative screen exposure over childhood was consistently linked to lower academic performance but not working memory. Early infancy emerged as a particularly sensitive period for screen time's impact on cognitive development.
