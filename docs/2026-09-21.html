ZuckOff Know when a camera is in the room

ZuckOff is an app that detects camera glasses nearby by listening for their Bluetooth signals and alerts you when devices like Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta, or Snap Spectacles are present. All processing happens locally on your phone with no account required, and the app provides background alerts, logging, and CSV export of detected devices. The app acknowledges its limitations: glasses are quietest when worn, so lack of detection doesn't prove no recording, and the creator is seeking more hardware to expand detection capabilities.

ZuckOff Is a Free App That Sees Meta Glasses Before They See You

ZuckOff is a free Bluetooth detection app that identifies Meta smart glasses nearby by detecting and fingerprinting their Bluetooth signals, detecting models like Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta. The app gained traction amid growing concerns about unsolicited filming, with roughly 7 million pairs of Meta smart glasses sold in 2025 and incidents of recorded content posted without consent. While ZuckOff can't determine if glasses are actively recording or identify the wearer, it provides rough proximity information and is difficult for Meta to legally challenge since it doesn't intercept signals.

North Korean hackers posed as recruiters with fake job listings to target software developers and IT professionals, infecting 30,000 devices across over 100 countries between December 2025 and July 2026, stealing $10 million in cryptocurrency and sensitive data like passwords and wallet information. The attackers tricked targets into downloading malicious files during fake technical interviews and coding assignments, then used malware to steal browser data, screenshots, and cryptocurrency wallets while gaining potential access to employers' networks. The group also conceals its identity to obtain remote IT work abroad, using AI face-swapping and stolen identity documents for impersonation, extortion, and intellectual property theft.
