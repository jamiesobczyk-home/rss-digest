The missing map of the sky

Astrophysicist Brice Ménard used Claude Science to create the first complete ultraviolet map of the entire sky by filling gaps in data from space telescopes, which had only observed about two-thirds of the sky due to technical limitations like ozone absorption and the risk of detector damage from bright stars. Claude orchestrated AI agents to gather and merge all available UV datasets, then predicted the missing portions using statistical techniques that would normally require weeks of meticulous work. The complete map serves as an educational tool for understanding the Milky Way's structure at ultraviolet wavelengths, revealing dust and stellar features invisible in other light spectra.
