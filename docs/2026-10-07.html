Permanent Daylight Saving Time Could Harm Sleep and Mental Health, Analysis Says

Canadian researchers argue that adopting permanent standard time would be preferable to permanent daylight saving time, as the latter would delay winter sunrises by an hour when mornings are already darkest, potentially harming sleep and mental health. Morning light is essential for maintaining circadian rhythm alignment, and delayed sunrises would counteract light therapy benefits for the estimated 1-3% of Canadians with seasonal affective disorder and 15% experiencing milder seasonal mood changes. Several Canadian provinces have already moved toward permanent daylight saving time, and while the researchers acknowledge this system has some advantages, they recommend more modest interventions like blackout curtains rather than the broader measures needed to address the effects of darker winter mornings.

Francis Halzen Wins Nobel Physics Prize For Antarctic 'Ghost Particle' Hunt

Francis Halzen won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics for developing the IceCube Neutrino Observatory beneath Antarctic ice, which detects high-energy neutrinos from cosmic sources like supermassive black holes. The discovery of neutrinos from these cosmic accelerators has enabled scientists to trace their origins, providing the first-ever evidence of cosmic ray sources and opening a new era of "multi-messenger" astronomy, since these nearly massless "ghost particles" travel undisturbed from their origins unlike charged cosmic rays.

Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2026 to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai

Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for discovering how to create homochiral reactions—chemical processes that produce only one mirror-image form of a molecule, solving a century-old chemistry mystery. Kagan's 1986 breakthrough enabled production of greater excesses of a single molecular form, while Soai achieved full homochirality in 2003, creating a reaction that produced only one of two possible mirror images. Their discoveries are crucial for pharmaceutical development, where only one molecular mirror image typically produces the desired biological effect.
