How Claude designed protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins

Claude generated 318,484 protein designs and selected 211 candidates to be experimentally tested as minibinders against scorpion neurotoxins; 17 met the criteria for successful binding. Scorpion stings kill over 3,250 people annually, mostly children in underserved regions, and this work aims to accelerate development of affordable antivenom treatments for this neglected tropical disease. All 211 designs and their experimental data are being released as a resource for the field, though converting these binders into safe, accessible drugs remains the next major challenge.

Whatever Happened to Fire Escapes

Fire escapes were external metal staircases developed in mid-1800s American cities as an evacuation alternative for tall tenement buildings prone to deadly fires. Though they became culturally iconic features of city skylines, there was never agreement on their actual effectiveness. Building codes—shaped by economics and the balance between safety and practicality—determined their use, and they have slowly disappeared as codes evolved.

Hacker News · 4:08 AM

Eight Bits of Trust; eMudhra's CA keys cracked in milliseconds

Eight eMudhra intermediate certificate authority private keys were compromised and found to be critically weak single-byte numbers (such as 4, 56, and 115) instead of proper 384-bit random values, allowing anyone to recover them in milliseconds through brute force. The affected keys powered six TLS and two code-signing certificates created in April 2025, which Chrome and Mozilla have already blocked. Unlike typical key compromises involving theft, these keys were effectively public from creation since anyone examining the public certificates could derive the private key in at most 255 computational steps.

The UN Environment Programme's 2026 Emissions Gap Report states that the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C warming limit is no longer achievable and overshoot is now inevitable.
