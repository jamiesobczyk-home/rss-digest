AI takes a swing at physics' biggest puzzle

Stanford physicist Venkatesa Chandrasekaran released an 83-page paper proposing a quantum theory of gravity developed with help from OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, which could reconcile Einstein's gravity with quantum mechanics at scales like black holes and the Big Bang. The theory uses light paths through spacetime to describe gravity while avoiding mathematical infinities, though it remains unreviewed and some physicists have dismissed it as a publicity stunt. If validated, it would demonstrate AI's capability to advance fundamental physics beyond solving math problems.

James T. Russell, Physicist Who Pioneered CD and DVD Technology, Dies At 95

James T. Russell, a physicist who invented the first digital-to-optical recording system in the 1960s-70s, has died at 95; his technology became the foundation for CD, DVD, and Blu-ray formats. Russell earned over 70 patents and solved the problem of vinyl record distortion by using lasers to read digital data, though his patents were later licensed by Sony and Philips without generating personal royalties. Despite transforming audio and data storage globally, Russell never became widely known for his pioneering work.

Scientists Used Seabirds To Track Hidden Ships

Scientists equipped seabirds with audio recorders and GPS trackers to detect fishing vessels operating with their location-broadcasting systems disabled, finding that roughly 32% of vessel interactions in the North Atlantic and 12% in the Mediterranean had their Automatic Identification System (AIS) turned off. While vessels may disable AIS for legitimate reasons like evading pirates, the widespread practice could indicate illegal fishing activities. The research reveals both broader patterns in commercial fishing operations and insights into seabird safety risks from interactions with these unseen vessels.
