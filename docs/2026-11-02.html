Supermarket freezers in Belgium and Sweden hit by suspected cyber-attack

Dozens of supermarkets across Belgium, Sweden, Luxembourg, and Ireland suffered a suspected cyber-attack that sabotaged refrigeration systems, with freezer temperatures maliciously set to 50°C (122°F), forcing the disposal of millions of euros worth of food. Approximately 40 Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg, several ICA and Coop locations in Sweden, and 13 SuperValu shops in Ireland were impacted, with one Delhaize branch reporting €100,000 in losses. The attack's perpetrators remain unknown, though Belgian and Swedish cyber-security agencies are investigating what follows a similar incident targeting French supermarkets last month.

China's First Frontier AI Export Controls: Mofcom Announcement No. 48

China's Ministry of Commerce issued Announcement No. 48 on October 16, 2026, adding 40 restricted technologies (23 in AI) to its export controls, including frontier model weights, agent architectures, and AI-enabled cyber capabilities with specific technical thresholds. The controls restrict exports of models meeting any of three criteria: 10^26 FLOPs of training compute, 2 trillion parameters, or top-10 global ranking, along with restrictions on advanced agent technologies for critical infrastructure and government use. An interim pre-export registration requirement is already in effect, while the catalogue additions are open for public comment until November 15, 2026.

From Charlemagne to the Kitchen Boy: Reading the Hamburg Rathaus

Hamburg's Rathaus functions as an architectural text encoding the city's history, from its copper patina revealing chemistry to the detailed figures, coats of arms, and the phoenix symbolizing rebirth after the Great Fire of 1842. The sixth iteration of the town hall, built between 1886–1897, features emperors Charlemagne and Friedrich I Barbarossa flanking the main entrance, the goddess Hammonia above them, and a phoenix sculpture commemorating the city's resurrection from devastating fires. The building survived World War II bombing raids, preserving every detail of its façade and remaining one of the few historic structures in central Hamburg's surviving landscape.

Rocky Linux Creator Built a New 'Not Linux' Kernel in 11 Days Using AI

Gregory Kurtzer, creator of Rocky Linux, built Ginux, a new operating system kernel in 11 days using an AI agent to write most of the code. Ginux is an independent Rust-first x86-64 kernel—not a Linux fork—that achieves Linux compatibility by implementing the Linux ABI, allowing existing Linux binaries to run unmodified.
