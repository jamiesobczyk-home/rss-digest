How to keep enjoying programming in a world of LLMs

A programmer argues for selective LLM use rather than complete automation, warning that delegating all coding to AI risks losing your programming skills, producing unmaintainable code that only LLMs can understand, and sacrificing the joy of writing code itself. The recommendation is to continue writing at least the enjoyable parts of your code manually while using LLMs strategically for tedious tasks, maintaining both your competence and your passion for programming. The author contends that apparent productivity gains from full LLM reliance come at the cost of long-term professional satisfaction and code quality.

Hacker News · 4:13 AM

Fifteen years later, the Apple Cards origin story

In 2011, Apple released the Cards app, initiated by Steve Jobs during a casual walk when he thought of sending thank you cards via iPhone, allowing users to design custom letterpress cards with photos and messages that Apple would mail on their behalf. The project, code-named Speed Racer, faced enormous technical challenges requiring coordination with a printing company and 1850s Heidelberg Letterpress shops, and was plagued by what insiders described as severe mismanagement and operational mayhem. The app launched during the final year of Steve Jobs' life and was eventually discontinued.

A single function Jev-like wrapper for LLMs, including vision models

The author created a Jev-like wrapper for LLMs that leverages token probability queries—using constrained generation and logprobs API parameters—to quickly classify data with minimal tokens, extending the approach to support vision models via a custom attachments field. A Python example demonstrates the technique with webcam frames, classifying presence of people, plants, indoor/outdoor settings, and brightness at ~1 FPS on local hardware and ~0.2 FPS with OpenAI's gpt-6-luna. The wrapper's flexibility allows conditions to be specified in plain text rather than relying on specialized computer vision models.
