How Zillow runs 100 million daily notifications on AWS Step Functions

Zillow consolidated its 100+ million daily notifications into a unified system using AWS Step Functions Express Workflows, replacing 10+ separate generator services with a configuration-driven Campaign Orchestrator and reusable Universal Content Generator modules. This architecture reduced workflow engine development from 32 weeks to 9 weeks and ongoing maintenance from 8 hours to 2 hours per week, while enabling faster product iteration and greater team self-service. The system achieves cost efficiency through batching optimization, where processing 10 messages in one batched execution costs 90% less than 10 separate runs while maintaining per-message isolation.

Why Writing Correct Software Is Hard

Writing correct software is fundamentally hard because programs and complete understanding—required for correctness—are at odds, governed by computational laws that cannot be avoided. The article traces this difficulty to foundational theoretical results, particularly Gödel's incompleteness theorems and the halting theorem, which demonstrate inherent limits to what can be formally proven and computed. While tools can assist, the cost of correctness is intrinsic to computation itself, making claims that tools can easily ensure correctness misleading.

Power Grids On 5 Continents Pulse Every 60s. Scientists Think They Know Why

Researchers have identified the cause of mysterious 60-second power grid pulses observed globally: uninterruptible power supplies performing routine automatic battery checks. These devices synchronize their internal clocks through external time signals, causing them to conduct their checks simultaneously across different buildings and countries. While individually insignificant, the combined electricity demand from thousands of synchronized systems creates measurable oscillations that ripple through entire power grids.
