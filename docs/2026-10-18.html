Managing open-weight model risk in enterprise deployments

Microsoft and NVIDIA published joint guidance on managing risks from open-weight AI models in enterprises, introducing a shared risk taxonomy across five categories (Cybersecurity, Data Protection, Content, Human-AI Interaction, and Bias) grounded in ISO and NIST frameworks. Risk depends on the model's use case, deployment context, and safeguards implemented—not solely on whether the model is open- or closed-weight. The paper maps evaluations to identified risks and provides guidance on implementing controls at model, inference, application, and operational layers, with plans to extend coverage and expand community contributions.

It's time to talk about TEEs

This article surveys multiple categories of security vulnerabilities in Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs) from major vendors like Intel and AMD, including bugs in enclave code, remote attestation forgery attacks, and physical interposition attacks on memory systems. The author argues that despite marketing claims, the complexity of modern CPU architectures and pervasive side-channels make TEE security claims unreliable, and users should reconsider whether to deploy TEE-based applications at all. While theoretical defenses exist, practical deployment requires careful management and creates significant security footguns.

Hacker News · 3:19 AM

Parser Combinators

Parser combinators are functions that transform unstructured text into structured data by taking a string and returning a parsed value plus the remaining unparsed string. Simple parsers can be combined sequentially using functions like pSeq to parse multiple elements in order, and transformed using pMap to reshape results into desired formats. This compositional approach enables building complex parsers from simpler, reusable units, as shown by examples parsing greeting strings and network ping output.

Notes on the Complex Roots of Unity

Complex roots of unity are numbers that equal 1 when raised to the nth power; while real roots are limited to 1 or -1, complex numbers yield n distinct solutions given by the formula z_k = e^(i2πk/n). These roots appear as equally spaced points around the unit circle, and multiplying a root by itself rotates it around the circle until it returns to 1.
