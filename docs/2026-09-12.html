Retrospectively Reverse-Engineering Apple's Neural Engine

Eileen Yoon is reverse-engineering Apple's M1 Neural Engine to document its complete internal architecture and the ML workload assumptions embedded in its silicon, three years after abandoning the project. Apple's decision to integrate ANE cores into GPU cores in the M5 processor marks the end of standalone NPUs, motivating this retrospective analysis of how the ANE evolved from CNN-optimized to transformer-era workloads. The ANE's 2048 parallel MAC lanes were architected for dense tensor operations with predictable data reuse patterns, but transformers broke these assumptions through autoregressive decoding, forcing a shift to different dataflow approaches.

Can Red-Light Therapy Treat Brain Injuries?

Researchers at the University of Utah are studying itPBM (intranasal transcranial photobiomodulation therapy), a medical-grade red-light therapy that shows promise in reducing brain inflammation and improving cognition, mood, anxiety, motor performance, and neural connectivity. A study of 26 BYU football players found that treated players showed no significant brain inflammation increase over a season, while untreated players developed widespread inflammation, along with improvements in depression, anxiety, attention, and motor control.
