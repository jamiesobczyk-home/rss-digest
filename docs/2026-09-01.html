California Lawmakers Greenlight Solar Panels You Can Plug Into the Wall

California lawmakers passed SB 868 to legalize plug-in "balcony" solar systems that residents can connect directly to standard wall outlets after online registration, with units capped at 1,200 watts and priced between $300-$2,200. The bill, which received bipartisan support, would exempt these devices from cumbersome interconnection processes and make solar accessible to renters, people with unsuitable roofs, and those unable to afford expensive rooftop installations. The systems must meet safety codes, be certified, and include anti-backfeed protection to prevent power from reaching the grid during outages.

Electrically Charged Raindrops Can Corrode Cars and Buildings

Researchers at the Max Planck Institute discovered that electrically charged water droplets—which form when moving water contacts different surfaces—can corrode protective coatings and underlying metals on cars and buildings. In experiments, thousands of charged droplets degraded Teflon and corroded copper sheets, while uncharged droplets caused no damage. The finding reveals that the electrical charge on water droplets, not just their chemical composition, drives corrosion.
