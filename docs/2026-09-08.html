Why Google Told Drivers to Drive a Longer Way On Purpose

Google's research team rerouted drivers in 10 U.S. cities via Google Maps to spread traffic across more corridors, achieving a 2% average speed increase and 0.7% shorter travel times, with the largest improvements on highways like those in Los Angeles (4.6% speed gain). The strategy avoided making changes too significant to trigger "induced demand," where major improvements encourage more drivers to use roads and erase gains. The experiment resulted in about 1,000 metric tons of annual CO2 emissions reductions, equivalent to 200 vehicles' emissions.
