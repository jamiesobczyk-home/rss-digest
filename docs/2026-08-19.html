Babies Born Under Sugar Rationing Grew Into Adults With Lower Cancer Risk

A study of over 64,000 people born in Britain found that those exposed to less sugar during their first 1,000 days—due to postwar rationing—had significantly lower cancer rates in adulthood, including 69% lower liver cancer and 36% lower breast cancer risk, and showed signs of slower biological aging. Most remarkably, people who experienced sugar rationing as infants continued consuming less sugar and had healthier diets at age 50, suggesting that early-life sweetness exposure shapes lifelong taste preferences.

Hacker News · 2:40 AM

Where Human Sleep Went Wrong

Evolutionary anthropologist David Samson explores the "sleep paradox": humans sleep fewer total hours than any other ape yet remain the most evolutionarily successful primates. His research with the Hadza tribe in Tanzania revealed that their fragmented, interrupted sleep contradicts both the "Paleo sleep hypothesis" and modern medical orthodoxy that views unbroken sleep as essential, despite the Hadza reporting high sleep satisfaction. The shift in human sleep occurred around 1.8 million years ago when humans descended from trees and learned to sleep safely on the ground through innovations like shelter and fire.

Top Album Releases Linked To Rise In Fatal Crashes

A Harvard Medical School study found that major album releases were linked to a 15.1% increase in US traffic fatalities on release days compared to surrounding days, equivalent to approximately 182 deaths across the 10 albums studied between 2017 and 2022. The rise in crashes is attributed to distracted driving, as accessing new music on smartphones requires multiple steps—unlocking the phone, opening the app, finding the release, and selecting a song—while unfamiliar, high-energy music also demands more driver attention. The increase in fatalities was particularly pronounced among younger drivers, male drivers, solo drivers, and those using cars with built-in infotainment systems.
