JIT bitmap text rendering for microcontrollers

A technique for fast bitmap text rendering on the RP2350 microcontroller uses a JIT compiler to generate highly optimized code on the fly for rendering strings. Because the chip has limited SRAM (520kB), the approach uses scanline-by-scanline video buffer rendering instead of a full framebuffer, operating under a tight performance budget of approximately 6 cycles per pixel. The renderer, written in ARM assembly, employs a display list architecture with double buffering to enable smooth, low-latency text rendering suited for the vic-2350 project, a re-envisioning of an 80s home computer.

Kirlian Photography: Building Your Own Equipment

Kirlian photography is a high-voltage contact printing technique developed by Semyon and Valentina Kirlian starting in 1939, where film placed between an object and a charged metal plate captures corona discharge patterns when exposed to electrical current. The process requires no camera or lens, making it a straightforward method to record the electrical discharge around objects. While paranormal claims have surrounded the technique—such as detecting illness or stress before visible symptoms appear—most observable effects can be explained by changes in electrical conductivity rather than metaphysical properties, though the "phantom leaf" phenomenon, where severed leaf portions appear ghostly in photographs, remains the most intriguing but inconsistently reproduced claim.
