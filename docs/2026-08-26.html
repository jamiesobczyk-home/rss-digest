Anthropic is launching a $5 million grant program to fund independent research into how AI impacts user wellbeing, providing grantees with funding, model access, and technical support to build open-source evaluations. The industry lacks clear standards for how AI should behave in sensitive contexts like mental health crises or when users seek emotional support, and wellbeing is difficult to assess because it requires understanding long-term conversation patterns and user history. Microsoft Azure Blog · 11:00 AM

The patch window is collapsing: Why security needs a new control plane

The traditional model where organizations have time to test and deploy patches before attackers exploit vulnerabilities no longer works—AI is accelerating attack timelines to hours while enterprises still need days or weeks to safely patch complex systems. Organizations now face a critical gap where they know which systems are vulnerable but cannot immediately patch them due to operational and business constraints. The industry is shifting toward network-level controls and adaptive security systems that can immediately reduce exposure during the patch window by constraining exploitable behavior, rather than relying on patching alone.

Scientists May Have Figured Out Why Dead Brains Don't Always Rot

Scientists have solved the mystery of why some human brains persist intact for thousands of years despite being among the first organs to decompose: in low-oxygen, wet environments, the chemical reactions that normally break down tissue instead bond proteins together, making the brain tougher and more resistant to decay. The study, published in the Journal of Proteome Research, found that free radicals—molecules typically destructive in oxygen-rich conditions—actually stabilize proteins when oxygen is scarce, turning the decay process into a preservation mechanism.

Hacker News · 3:59 AM

Value Classes Still Need Compiler Sympathy

JDK 28 integrates JEP 401's value classes as a preview feature, enabling JVM optimizations like flattening and scalarization by removing object identity requirements, but a "value all the classes" approach risks performance issues. Value classes gain their main advantage through immutability: final fields can use non-atomic flattened layouts without atomic update constraints, allowing large values like FourLongs (32 bytes) to be optimally stored within records, whereas mutable classes force reference layouts. The JVM's optimization effectiveness depends on carefully considering when flattening is beneficial and understanding that representation conversion between flattened and reference forms can create performance penalties when methods interact.
